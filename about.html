Why I Built This

My daughter’s grades told only part of the story.

A Note From Me

My daughter earned straight A’s in middle and high school. Then she reached college and struggled. Memorizing had helped her pass, but college asked her to question ideas, weigh evidence, and apply what she knew to problems she had not seen before.

Her experience made me look more closely at what a report card can leave unanswered. Can a child explain an idea in their own words? Can they use it when the question changes? Can they work through it without someone guiding each step? Can they say why an answer makes sense, not only what it is?

I began creating learning tools for my own children, and The Learner Project grew from that work.

I bring more than 20 years of experience working with children, including in special education classrooms in DeKalb County and Gwinnett County schools, and more than five years in applied behavior analysis (ABA). Those experiences taught me to observe carefully: how a child approaches a task, where confusion begins, and what helps them move forward.

That attention guides the lessons I create. I want your child to understand what they are learning well enough to explain it, use it in a new situation, and teach someone else. That is what we work toward.

Mrs. Merissa

Merissa Wilson, founder of The Learner Project

How I Teach

I create personalized digital lessons around what your child needs to learn. I review their responses to see how they reached an answer, where they needed help, and what to teach next. I keep you informed about what your child can do independently, where they still need support, and what we are working on next.

Each lesson explains the thinking in manageable steps, then provides practice with guidance and feedback. Once the idea makes sense, I introduce strategies and shortcuts, including when and why to use them. As your child shows understanding, I reduce the support and ask them to try new questions on their own. Later checks show what they can still explain and use after time has passed.

I use ABA teaching techniques to break skills into small steps, give the right amount of help, and step back as your child gains confidence. This is academic learning support, not ABA therapy.

Lessons follow practices supported by education research: clear step-by-step teaching, practice with feedback, and review spread out over time so learning lasts. They align with your child’s state standards, including Georgia’s, and draw on teaching approaches from top-performing school systems around the world, which focus on deep understanding over memorizing.

Reading sessions use systematic phonics, teaching the connections between letters and sounds in a planned sequence. All tutors are trained in The Learner Project’s teaching method, with ongoing guidance and oversight from me.
