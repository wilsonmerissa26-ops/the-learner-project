Why I built this

I know what it looks like when grades hide the gap.

Ms. MerissaMerissa Wilson, founder of The Learner Project

My daughter made straight A's in middle and high school. Then she got to college and struggled. She had learned to memorize well enough to pass, and college asked her to understand.

That's when I saw that a report card can't tell you whether a child truly knows something. I started building learning tools for my own children, and The Learner Project grew from that work.

My goal is a child who can explain what they learned, use it somewhere new, and teach it to someone else. That's the standard every lesson is held to. I've worked with children for more than 20 years, including more than five years in ABA.

The unexpected pass

Maya usually passed to the same teammate. During the game, a defender moved to block that pass. Maya spotted Jordan alone in the corner and sent the ball to Jordan instead.

Why did Maya change where she passed?

First, notice the change: a defender blocked the usual pass. Then connect it to the next detail: Jordan was open. Together, those details explain why Maya chose a different pass.

Pip takes the high path

Rain filled the low path beside the creek. Pip watched the water cover the stones where she usually crossed. She turned and led her cubs up the hill.

Why did Pip lead her cubs up the hill?

First, notice the problem: water covered the usual crossing. Then connect it to Pip’s choice to go uphill. The details support an answer about avoiding the flooded path, rather than simply saying that Pip likes hills.

Why it matters now

The world is changing. Memorizing isn't enough anymore.

Answers are easier to get than ever. A phone can solve a math problem or summarize a chapter in seconds. What your child needs now is the ability to think. That means figuring out what a question is really asking, explaining why an answer is right, catching their own mistakes, and using what they know in a situation they've never seen.

That's critical thinking, and it's what The Learner Project is built to teach.

The method

Digital lessons and learning games, adjusted by a real person.

Your child learns at home, at their own pace, through digital lessons and learning games that practice the skills in their plan. I create every lesson, and I review their completed work and adjust their plan as they go. Each lesson shows the thinking, gives practice with help, and then asks your child to try new questions without hints. Later checks help me see what they can still use. The digital lessons aren't live. For children learning to read, live reading and phonics sessions are a separate option.

I use ABA teaching techniques to break skills into small steps, give the right amount of help, and step back as your child gets more confident. Lessons also draw on teaching methods backed by education research. This is academic learning support, not ABA therapy.

Find the real breakdown

I look for where learning actually breaks down: understanding what was read, vocabulary, reasoning, following steps, remembering, or using a skill somewhere new. If reading the words themselves is the problem, we'll talk about separate live reading support.

Teach the thinking first

Before asking for answers, lessons show your child what to notice, why it matters, and what to do next. Then the help steps back, a little at a time.

Check it without help

I ask your child to explain, compare, correct mistakes, and use the skill later without being walked through every step.

Explain it in their own words

I pay attention to whether your child can say why an answer is right, not just give the answer.

Questions parents ask

Straight answers.

What is The Learner Project?

A personalized digital learning program built around your child's needs. The goal is for your child to understand what they're learning, use it on their own, and explain how they got their answers.

Who is it for?

Elementary, middle, and high school students who need extra support in reading, vocabulary, spelling, math, science, or a mix of subjects. That includes children who struggle with reading, children falling behind in math, children who've tried everything, and children whose good grades hide a gap.

What makes it different?

A wrong answer doesn't always tell the whole story. A child might not know the skill, might misunderstand the directions, or might get stuck on the reading. I look beyond the score to find where learning breaks down. A child struggling with fractions, for example, may first need help understanding equal parts. Your child's work then guides what comes next: a different explanation, more practice, or a new challenge.

How do you figure out what my child needs?

I start with what you share and with short activities aimed at the skills you're concerned about. I look at what your child can do on their own, where they get stuck, and what kind of help moves them forward. Schoolwork you choose to share can help guide the plan too.

Is every child doing the same lessons?

No. Each child gets a customized plan. Two children in the same grade may need different starting points, explanations, and practice.

Are there live sessions?

The digital program isn't live. Your child does lessons and activities at home on a laptop or tablet with an internet connection. I review their completed work and contact you about progress or concerns. For children learning to read, live reading and phonics sessions are offered separately. Ask about availability.

How much time does it take?

Lessons are short, and learning games give extra practice on the skills in your child's plan. You don't need to teach the lessons. Your part is making sure they get done. Once I know your child, I'll suggest a weekly rhythm.

Is it all games?

No. Lessons and practice come first. Learning games are included when they practice the skill your child is working on.

Will my child actually enjoy it?

That's the goal. I build lessons around what your child already loves, from the stories they read to the examples in their math and the themes of their games. If something isn't clicking, I change it.

What if my child gets the right answer by guessing?

One right answer doesn't mean a skill is understood. Follow-up questions, showing their work, and similar problems help check whether your child really understands.

Can this help when reading affects other subjects?

Yes. Reading difficulties can make word problems, science questions, and even directions harder. Your child's plan can address those connected needs.

My child has an IEP, ADHD, or dyslexia. Can they join?

Possibly. Tell me what helps your child learn and what support they already receive, and we'll talk about fit. This is academic learning support. It doesn't diagnose, and it doesn't replace school services or therapy. For a child who is still learning to sound out words, live reading and phonics sessions may be the better fit.

What if my child needs help sounding out words?

Tell me during intake. Understanding what you read and sounding out words are different skills, and they need different kinds of teaching. We can talk about whether live reading and phonics sessions are a good fit, if spots are available.

Will I know how my child is doing?

Yes. I review completed work and share updates on progress, areas that still need support, and next steps. You can share concerns along the way too.

Is this ABA therapy?

No. I use ABA teaching techniques to break skills into small steps, give the right amount of help, and step back as your child gets more confident. The Learner Project is academic learning support, not ABA therapy. It doesn't replace school, therapy, or special education services.

How do I ask about class support or another service?

These are separate paid services. Check the ones you're interested in on the waitlist form, and I'll confirm availability, what's included, and the price before you decide. Expressing interest doesn't enroll or charge you.

How does the free pilot work?

The first pilot includes two students for 30 days at no cost, and each gets a customized learning plan. Before your child starts, we'll put their included subjects and support in writing. Before the pilot ends, I'll share what I recommend and the price. Every plan is built around your child, so the price depends on what they need. You're free to stop at the end of the pilot, and nothing renews automatically.

What happens after I apply?

I'll reply by email within 48 to 72 hours. If it looks like a good fit, we'll set up a short call before anything starts. If it isn't the right fit right now, you're welcome to join the waitlist.

What if the pilot spots are filled?

Join the waitlist. When I consider future spots, I review the requests and may contact families whose needs fit. If I offer a spot, I'll explain what's included and any price before you decide. You can also email me with questions at thelearnerproject2026@gmail.com.
